# FAO–UNESCO: three texture groups (1974)

## Source and scope

Primary source: [FAO-Unesco, Soil Map of the World, Volume I: Legend
(1974)](https://www.fao.org/4/as360e/as360e.pdf), printed pages 4–5
(PDF pages 11–12). The numerical definitions and triangle are on printed page 5.

This is the original three-group map legend, not the detailed triangle in
later FAO Guidelines for Soil Description or the WRB soil classification.
Edition matters: do not substitute limits from later summaries under this ID.
The legend applies the grouping to the upper 30 cm of the dominant soil in a
map association. The app classifies the supplied sample; it does not verify
sampling depth or assign a soil taxonomic unit.

## Definition

Selector: **FAO–UNESCO · 1974 · 3 groups**.
Stable export ID: `FAO-UNESCO (1974, 3 groups)`.

The original polygons in `R/classifications/fao_1974.R` follow these interiors:

| Group | Numerical limits |
| --- | --- |
| Coarse | Clay below 18%, sand above 65% |
| Medium | Clay below 35%, outside the coarse region |
| Fine | Clay above 35% |

The medium region extends to the sand/clay edge at (82% sand, 18% clay).
This follows the source's explicit extension for clay contents of at least
18%, and produces the step in the coarse/medium boundary shown in its figure.
There is no diagonal interpolation between the 65% sand and 18% clay limits.

All polygons include their edges for candidate detection. As with the other
views, a shared edge returns `boundary`, its candidates and no single class,
rather than choosing an inclusive inequality. Pure sand, silt and clay have
unique candidates Coarse, Medium and Fine respectively.

## Input convention

Use fine-earth percentages (<2 mm), with sand 0.05–2 mm, silt 0.002–0.05 mm
and clay <0.002 mm. This input convention follows [FAO's soil-texture training
manual, section 6.7](https://www.fao.org/fishery/static/FAO_Training/FAO_Training/General/x6706e/x6706e06.htm),
which explicitly describes these laboratory fractions. The 1974 legend supplies
the grouping limits; its page 5 does not independently state all size cutoffs.
Do not confuse this convention with the 0.063 mm sand/silt cutoff shown in
later FAO guidelines. Selecting this view cannot convert laboratory fractions.

## Validation

- `tests/fixtures/fao_1974.csv`: reference interiors for all three groups and
  both sides of the step.
- `tests/fao_1974.R`: 10,000 reproducible random compositions checked against
  independent numerical rules, exact step/fine boundaries, nearby values,
  outer corners, example samples, Shiny results, source metadata and CSV exports.
- Shared tests discover the definition automatically and check total area,
  5,151 whole-percentage compositions, absence of gaps/overlaps, polygon-order
  independence and warning-free rendering with and without samples.

An upload example is in `examples/fao_1974_samples.csv`.
