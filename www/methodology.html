Methodology & limitations

This research prototype plots sand, silt, and clay percentages against New Zealand, USDA, Australian, Brazilian, Canadian, FAO–UNESCO, UK, French GEPPA, and German BK94 texture polygons. The original three systems retain their project coordinates; their source verification is ongoing.

Brazil — SiBCS 2025

Two views implement the 6th edition of the Sistema Brasileiro de Classificação de Solos: 13 detailed classes (Figure 1.1, printed page 55) and five texture groups (Figure 1.2, printed pages 55–56). Class names are in Portuguese; exported system names identify the edition and view.

Use percentages of the fine-earth fraction: sand 0.05–2 mm, silt 0.002–0.05 mm, and clay <0.002 mm. The manual uses g/kg; divide those values by ten for this app. Changing the selector does not convert measurements from another particle-size convention.

These views follow the shared-boundary candidate policy below, rather than forcing a single class at exact published limits such as 60% clay. Full implementation and validation details are in docs/classifications/brazil.md.

Canada — CSSC third edition (1998)

The Canadian view contains 13 main classes, including Heavy Clay above 60% clay, based on Chapter 17, Figure 42 and the AAFC numerical class glossary. It excludes sand-size subclasses and coarse-fragment modifiers. Inputs use fine-earth percentages with a 0.05 mm sand/silt cutoff. Shared boundaries follow the same candidate policy as other views.

Exports identify Canada (CSSC 1998, 13 classes). Full notes are in docs/classifications/canada.md.

FAO–UNESCO — three groups (1974)

The historical Soil Map of the World legend provides Coarse, Medium and Fine groups. The coarse/medium boundary has a step at 18% clay and 65% sand; the fine group starts above 35% clay. This implements the 1974 map grouping, not the later detailed FAO triangle. The original map applies these groups to the upper 30 cm; the app classifies the supplied composition without inferring sampling depth.

Use fine-earth percentages with sand 0.05–2 mm, silt 0.002–0.05 mm and clay <0.002 mm. Changing systems does not convert particle-size measurements. Shared edges follow the candidate policy below.

Exports identify FAO-UNESCO (1974, 3 groups). Full notes are in docs/classifications/fao_1974.md.

UK — mineral soils (TIN037, 2008)

The UK view follows Natural England TIN037, first edition (21 February 2008), Figure 1. It contains the 11 main mineral-soil classes, including Sandy Silt Loam. Organic mineral, peat and calcareous variants, sand-size prefixes and medium/heavy clay-loam subdivisions are excluded. The app does not infer organic matter or carbonate content from the supplied composition.

Use sand 0.06–2 mm, silt 0.002–0.06 mm and clay <0.002 mm. This sand/silt cutoff differs from 0.05 mm systems; changing the selector does not convert laboratory measurements. Shared boundaries retain the candidate policy below.

Exports identify UK (TIN037 2008, 11 classes). Full notes are in docs/classifications/uk_tin037.md.

France — GEPPA 17 classes (1963)

The French view uses the detailed GEPPA diagram reproduced by INRAE, with numerical vertices transcribed from Moeys and contributors' soiltexture 1.5.3 definition. The original right-triangle coordinates are shown in the app's equilateral layout. The original 1963 publication has not been independently retrieved; source and numerical precision notes are recorded in docs/classifications/france_geppa.md.

Labels and exports preserve French names and case-sensitive codes: As (Argile sableuse) and AS (Argilo-sableux) are distinct. This is not the Aisne/Jamagne triangle or the grouped GEPPA views.

Use fine-earth percentages with sand 0.05–2 mm, silt 0.002–0.05 mm and clay <0.002 mm. Switching systems does not convert laboratory fractions. Shared edges retain the candidate policy below. Exports identify France (GEPPA 1963, 17 classes).

Germany — BK94 31 classes (1994)

The German view implements the fourth-edition Bodenkundliche Kartieranleitung triangle, using the numerical DE.BK94.TT transcription in Moeys and contributors' soiltexture 1.5.3. The original 1994 book has not been independently retrieved. This option identifies its historical edition and does not represent later German editions.

Use fine-earth percentages with sand 0.063–2 mm, silt (Schluff) 0.002–0.063 mm and clay (Ton) <0.002 mm. Switching systems does not convert particle-size measurements. The plot uses short codes for readability; expand the class label key under About & methodology for German names. Results and exports retain both.

Exports identify Germany (BK94 1994, 31 classes). Source, precision and scope notes are in docs/classifications/germany_bk94.md. Shared edges follow the candidate policy below; package comparison remains future work.

How classes are reported

ResultMeaning
ClassifiedA single polygon interior matches.
BoundaryA point is on an edge or vertex. A unique candidate receives its class; multiple candidates are listed without a single assigned class.
OverlapMore than one polygon interior matches. Candidates are listed without selecting a class.
UnclassifiedNo polygon matches.

Edge detection uses a numerical tolerance of 10−10 in sand/clay fraction coordinates. This is not a tolerance for laboratory measurement error. Input percentages must total 100 within 10−8 percentage points; values are never automatically normalised.

Known geometry issue

The Australian polygons contain an overlap between Sand and Sandy Loam. A composition of 90.5% sand, 1.3% silt, and 8.2% clay matches both interiors. The app flags this overlap; the polygon coordinates have not been corrected without source verification.
