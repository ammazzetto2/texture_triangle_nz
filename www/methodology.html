Methodology & limitations

This research prototype plots sand, silt, and clay percentages against New Zealand, USDA, Australian, Brazilian, Canadian, FAO–UNESCO, UK, French GEPPA and Aisne/Jamagne, German BK94, SEA74 and TGL85, Belgian, European HYPRES, Romanian SRTS 2003, ISSS, historical USDA 1911, and Polish PTG 2008 texture polygons. The original three systems retain their project coordinates; their source verification is ongoing.

Brazil — 13 classes and five groups

The selector identifies the detailed view with 2013 (SiBCS) and the five-group view with 1996 (Lemos and Santos). These are reference years; the current polygon implementation and export identifiers retain their documented SiBCS 2025 provenance.

Two views implement the 6th edition of the Sistema Brasileiro de Classificação de Solos: 13 detailed classes (Figure 1.1, printed page 55) and five texture groups (Figure 1.2, printed pages 55–56). Class names are in Portuguese; exported system names identify the edition and view.

Use percentages of the fine-earth fraction: sand 0.05–2 mm, silt 0.002–0.05 mm, and clay <0.002 mm. The manual uses g/kg; divide those values by ten for this app. Changing the selector does not convert measurements from another particle-size convention.

These views follow the shared-boundary candidate policy below, rather than forcing a single class at exact published limits such as 60% clay. Full implementation and validation details are in docs/classifications/brazil.md.

Canada — CSSC third edition (1998)

The Canadian view contains 13 main classes, including Heavy Clay above 60% clay, based on Chapter 17, Figure 42 and the AAFC numerical class glossary. It excludes sand-size subclasses and coarse-fragment modifiers. Inputs use fine-earth percentages with a 0.05 mm sand/silt cutoff. Shared boundaries follow the same candidate policy as other views.

Exports identify Canada (CSSC 1998, 13 classes). Full notes are in docs/classifications/canada.md.

FAO–UNESCO — three groups (1974)

The historical Soil Map of the World legend provides Coarse, Medium and Fine groups. The coarse/medium boundary has a step at 18% clay and 65% sand; the fine group starts above 35% clay. This implements the 1974 map grouping, not the later detailed FAO triangle. The original map applies these groups to the upper 30 cm; the app classifies the supplied composition without inferring sampling depth.

Use fine-earth percentages with sand 0.05–2 mm, silt 0.002–0.05 mm and clay <0.002 mm. Changing systems does not convert particle-size measurements. Shared edges follow the candidate policy below.

Exports identify FAO-UNESCO (1974, 3 groups). Full notes are in docs/classifications/fao_1974.md.

UK — mineral soils (TIN037, 2008)

The UK view follows Natural England TIN037, first edition (21 February 2008), Figure 1. It contains the 11 main mineral-soil classes, including Sandy Silt Loam. Organic mineral, peat and calcareous variants, sand-size prefixes and medium/heavy clay-loam subdivisions are excluded. The app does not infer organic matter or carbonate content from the supplied composition.

Use sand 0.06–2 mm, silt 0.002–0.06 mm and clay <0.002 mm. This sand/silt cutoff differs from 0.05 mm systems; changing the selector does not convert laboratory measurements. Shared boundaries retain the candidate policy below.

Exports identify UK (TIN037 2008, 11 classes). Full notes are in docs/classifications/uk_tin037.md.

France — GEPPA 17 classes (1963)

The French view uses the detailed GEPPA diagram reproduced by INRAE, with numerical vertices transcribed from Moeys and contributors' soiltexture 1.5.3 definition. The original right-triangle coordinates are shown in the app's equilateral layout. The original 1963 publication has not been independently retrieved; source and numerical precision notes are recorded in docs/classifications/france_geppa.md.

Labels and exports preserve French names and case-sensitive codes: As (Argile sableuse) and AS (Argilo-sableux) are distinct. This is not the Aisne/Jamagne triangle or the grouped GEPPA views.

Use fine-earth percentages with sand 0.05–2 mm, silt 0.002–0.05 mm and clay <0.002 mm. Switching systems does not convert laboratory fractions. Shared edges retain the candidate policy below. Exports identify France (GEPPA 1963, 17 classes).

Germany — BK94 31 classes (1994)

The German view implements the fourth-edition Bodenkundliche Kartieranleitung triangle, using the numerical DE.BK94.TT transcription in Moeys and contributors' soiltexture 1.5.3. The original 1994 book has not been independently retrieved. This option identifies its historical edition and does not represent later German editions.

Use fine-earth percentages with sand 0.063–2 mm, silt (Schluff) 0.002–0.063 mm and clay (Ton) <0.002 mm. Switching systems does not convert particle-size measurements. The plot uses short codes for readability; expand the class label key under About & methodology for German names. Results and exports retain both.

Exports identify Germany (BK94 1994, 31 classes). Source, precision and scope notes are in docs/classifications/germany_bk94.md. Shared edges follow the candidate policy below; package comparison remains future work.

Belgium — seven mineral-soil classes

The Belgian triangle contains Z, S, P, L, A, E and U. The Flemish field guide's Figure 4.8 documents the diagram; numerical coordinates are transcribed from Moeys and contributors' soiltexture 1.5.3, BE.TT. The plot shows codes; the expandable class label key, results and exports retain French and Dutch names.

Use fine-earth percentages with sand 0.05–2 mm, silt (limon / leem) 0.002–0.05 mm and clay <0.002 mm. Switching systems does not convert measurements. This view does not infer drainage, profile development, peat or other soil-map attributes.

Exports identify Belgium (BE.TT 1.5.3, 7 classes); the version identifies the numerical transcription, not a publication year. Source and scope notes are in docs/classifications/belgium.md. Shared edges follow the candidate policy below; package comparisons remain future work.

Europe — HYPRES five mineral texture classes

This view provides Coarse, Medium, Medium fine, Fine and Very fine, using the numerical HYPRES.TT transcription in Moeys and contributors' soiltexture 1.5.3. ESDAC documents the database context and fraction convention. It is separate from the FAO–UNESCO 1974 three-group triangle: it adds Medium fine and Very fine regions.

Use fine-earth percentages with sand 0.05–2 mm, silt 0.002–0.05 mm and clay <0.002 mm. Switching systems does not convert laboratory fractions. This option supplies mineral texture classes only; it does not infer organic horizons, topsoil/subsoil categories or hydraulic properties.

Exports identify Europe (HYPRES.TT 1.5.3, 5 classes); the version identifies the numerical transcription. Source and scope notes are in docs/classifications/europe_hypres.md. Shared edges follow the candidate policy below; package comparison remains future work.

France — Aisne/Jamagne 15 classes

This is the modified 15-class Aisne/Jamagne triangle, documented by INRAE's Figure 2 and numerically transcribed from Moeys and contributors' soiltexture 1.5.3, FR.AISNE.TT. It is separate from GEPPA and does not claim to reproduce the original 1967 edition unchanged.

Use fine-earth percentages with sand 0.05–2 mm, silt 0.002–0.05 mm and clay <0.002 mm. The plot shows standard codes; expand the class label key for French names. Results and exports retain both. Switching views does not convert measurements.

Exports identify France (Aisne-Jamagne, FR.AISNE.TT 1.5.3, 15 classes). Source and scope notes are in docs/classifications/france_aisne.md. Shared edges follow the candidate policy below; package comparison remains future work.

Romania — SRTS 2003 texture view (15 regions)

This view follows the ROM.TT numerical transcription in Moeys and contributors' soiltexture 1.5.3, labelled SRTS 2003 and contributed by Bogdan Rosca. It has 15 regions: SG+SM+SF, UG+UM+UF and NG+NM+NF each combine coarse, medium and fine sand subclasses. Sand, silt and clay totals cannot distinguish those subclasses, so results retain the combined codes.

Use fine-earth percentages with sand (nisip) 0.02–2 mm, silt (praf) 0.002–0.02 mm and clay (argilă) <0.002 mm. Switching systems does not convert measurements. Plot codes link to Romanian names in the expandable key. This option supplies texture only, not full SRTS soil taxonomy.

Exports identify Romania (SRTS 2003, ROM.TT 1.5.3, 15 regions). Source, grouping and continuous-boundary notes are in docs/classifications/romania_srts2003.md. The original 2003 publication has not been independently retrieved; package comparisons remain future work.

ISSS — 12 texture classes

This view follows the ISSS.TT numerical transcription in Moeys and contributors' soiltexture 1.5.3, contributed by Wei Shangguan after Verheye and Ameryckx (1984). Its 12 classes include Heavy clay and Light clay. Standard plot codes link to full English names in the expandable key; results and exports preserve both.

Use fine-earth percentages with sand 0.02–2 mm, silt 0.002–0.02 mm and clay <0.002 mm. Switching systems does not convert laboratory measurements. This is a distinct texture triangle, not a USDA alias or merely a particle-size convention.

Exports identify ISSS (ISSS.TT 1.5.3, 12 classes). Source and scope notes are in docs/classifications/isss.md. The original 1984 article has not been independently retrieved; package comparison remains future work. Shared edges follow the candidate policy below.

Germany — historical SEA74 and TGL85

SEA74 (1974) provides 13 classes for forest soils; TGL85 (TGL 24300-05:1985-06) provides 14 for agricultural soils. Numerical coordinates follow DE.SEA74.TT and DE.TGL85.TT in Moeys and contributors' soiltexture 1.5.3. These historical options are separate from BK94. The original standards have not been independently retrieved.

Use fine-earth percentages with sand 0.063–2 mm, silt 0.002–0.063 mm and clay <0.002 mm. Switching systems does not convert measurements. The plot uses codes; the key, results and CSV preserve German names. TGL85 distinguishes l''S, l'S and lS.

Exports identify each scheme, historical year and numerical transcription version. SEA74's upper Ton region begins above 45% clay, while TGL85 uses 50%. Both retain their transcribed 18% clay breaks. Source and scope notes are in docs/classifications/germany_sea74.md and docs/classifications/germany_tgl85.md. Shared boundaries retain the candidate policy below; package comparisons remain future work.

USDA — historical Whitney 1911 (eight classes)

This historical option follows the USDA1911 numerical transcription in Moeys and contributors' soiltexture 1.5.3, credited to Nic Jelinski. It is separate from the existing USDA option. The original Whitney publication has not been independently retrieved.

Use historical clay <0.005 mm, silt 0.005–0.05 mm and sand 0.05–2 mm. The clay/silt cutoff differs from modern USDA's 0.002 mm. Switching systems does not convert laboratory measurements.

Source-label caveat: the package calls its silt-side SaCL region “Sandy Clay Loam.” This implementation retains that source code, name and geometry pending review of the original historical publication. The key, results and exports preserve source names.

Exports identify USDA 1911 (Whitney, USDA1911 1.5.3, 8 classes). Full notes are in docs/classifications/usda_1911.md. Shared edges follow the candidate policy below; package comparison remains future work.

Years in the classification selector

The formerly undated options now show reference publication years: New Zealand 1995 (Milne et al.), USDA 1951 (Soil Survey Staff), Belgium 1984 (Verheye and Ameryckx), Aisne/Jamagne 1967, and Australia 2018 (Pluske, Boggs and Leopold). These are explicitly marked “reference” in the selector.

Reference dates do not certify the exact edition behind legacy polygon coordinates. The 15-class Aisne view includes later modifications, and the 2018 Australian published diagram differs from this app's legacy coordinates. The precise Australian variant year remains unresolved. Full evidence is recorded in docs/classification-years.md; the coordinates and export IDs are unchanged.

Poland — PTG 2008, 16 texture classes

This view implements PTG 2008, adopted by the Polish Society of Soil Science on 5 March 2008 and published in 2009. Table 2 and Figure 1 of the original publication document the main triangle. Numerical geometry follows PTG_2008.TT in soiltexture 1.5.3; the app uses local static coordinates without a package dependency.

Use fine-earth mass percentages with sand (piasek) 0.05–2 mm, silt (pył) 0.002–0.05 mm and clay (ił) <0.002 mm. This is separate from older Polish triangles with other fraction limits. Switching systems does not convert measurements. Sand-size subdivisions, coarse-fragment classes and full soil taxonomy are excluded.

Plot codes link to Polish names in the expandable key; exports preserve codes and diacritics. Exports identify Poland (PTG 2008, PTG_2008.TT 1.5.3, 16 classes). Full notes are in docs/classifications/poland_ptg2008.md. Shared edges follow the candidate policy below; independent package comparisons remain future work.

How classes are reported

ResultMeaning
ClassifiedA single polygon interior matches.
BoundaryA point is on an edge or vertex. A unique candidate receives its class; multiple candidates are listed without a single assigned class.
OverlapMore than one polygon interior matches. Candidates are listed without selecting a class.
UnclassifiedNo polygon matches.

Edge detection uses a numerical tolerance of 10−10 in sand/clay fraction coordinates. This is not a tolerance for laboratory measurement error. Input percentages must total 100 within 10−8 percentage points; values are never automatically normalised.

Known geometry issue

The Australian polygons contain an overlap between Sand and Sandy Loam. A composition of 90.5% sand, 1.3% silt, and 8.2% clay matches both interiors. The app flags this overlap; the polygon coordinates have not been corrected without source verification.
